Map composition families

F1 · Portrait spinePopulation questions across the country. Map ≈ 35–40%, left; rows on paper bound by leaders.
F2 · Full route + bottom stripSingle-transport questions about progress/stops. Map ≈ 85%; the strip keeps distance true and re-scopes on zoom.
F3 · Route + edge time'When will it be where.' Time on the vertical edge in the direction of travel.
F4 · Corridor zoomDriver legality, range, along-road decisions. Camera on the 100 km that matter; the day crosses the map.
F5 · Object docked to mapVehicle questions with a place (range, gates). The object keeps its drawing, with a leader to the marker.
F6 · Segment + sectionRestrictions. Street-level segment + a detail bubble with the physical fact.
F7 · Stop at street levelArrival, approach, dock. Minute ticks along the street line; window as a ring or a strip.
F8 · Map-free with locatorDocuments, rules without place, comparisons. The map collapses to a locator line.

Map prominence rules

  • Prominence follows the question, not the anchor: the same Driver can be 0%, 20% or 100% map.
  • Grow the map only when a lens adds a place-bound fact (route, stop, restriction segment, range).
  • Choose the frame by the geometry: N–S questions get portrait (F1/F3); along-road questions get a corridor (F4); local questions get the street (F6/F7).
  • Never landscape-fit Portugal: it wastes 50–80% on sea and Spain.
  • Never place a panel on the map. Content either rides an edge that shares an axis with the map, or binds to geometry with a leader.

Transition rules

  • Camera and layout move on the same curve and duration (0.9 s); annotations fade out and return after the camera settles.
  • Street-level detail is hidden while the camera moves and appears once it settles (semantic zoom; also a performance requirement).
  • Objects keep identity: a drawing shrinks into its marker and grows back from it (S-C).
  • Strips survive zoom by re-scoping their km range rather than disappearing (S-B).
  • Anchor switch with the map open: the map does not move; line styling and the docked object change (M-07).
  • Esc restores the exact previous geometry (camera fit, strip scale, block rects).
  • Time-direction alignment: an edge time axis runs in the direction of travel (Faro→Porto north ⇒ time up), otherwise leaders cross.
  • Isolate by ghosting: other routes recede to dashed grey; never hide context the user may still need.

When the map should disappear

  • The question has no place: documents, legal counters without a road, comparisons of non-spatial facts.
  • It collapses into a one-line locator ('A1 · km 282 · 10:05') that keeps continuity at zero cost.
  • Twin maps for non-spatial decisions are a smell (G-22).

When the map should dominate

  • Where must X happen (legal stop, refuel, detour)?
  • What happens near this place (approach, segment, brushing)?
  • Who can reach this place by when (assignment)?
  • Several transports interacting in space (night ops, closures).

Related entities without panels

  • Leader lines from rows/strips to markers (A1, A3).
  • Docked technical objects with a leader (A5).
  • Chips anchored where a condition is decided (G-20), with physical conditions on the object, not on the map.
  • Detail bubbles (CAD 'detail A') for physical facts at a place (A7).
  • Edge instruments only when they share the map's axis (bottom km strip, vertical time); otherwise not at all.
