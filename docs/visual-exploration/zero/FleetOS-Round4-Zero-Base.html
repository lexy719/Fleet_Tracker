Patterns that should never enter FleetOS

  • Circular time (dials, rings, 24h clocks) for dispatch decisions (ZD-01, ZD-08, ZO-09).
  • Isometric or perspective views that occlude data (ZD-06).
  • Skeuomorphic containers: folders, folded maps, file tabs (ZT-07, ZR-07).
  • Tabs as the primary structure, in any disguise.
  • Landscape maps of Portugal, map tiles beside content, floating white cards on maps.
  • Tables and registers as destinations (ZF-07, ZX-07). Fine as exports, never as the screen.
  • Distortions of distance or time for decoration (diagonal, folds, perspective).
  • Dark cockpit aesthetics on the dispatcher's desk; dark is a night-shift mode at most.
  • Colour on normal states. Grey is normal; colour is exception (ISA-101).
  • Rotated text and rotated objects as layout devices (ZV-08).

The final question

If we threw away every current screen but kept the product intelligence, what should we rebuild?

Seven screens, one language. Each domain gets a single signature canvas in its native form: the road for the driver, the string chart with its corridor for the route, the drawing sheet for the vehicle, the fork in time for the transport, the paper timeline for documents, the two futures for restrictions and the latitude metro for the operation.

Three shared axes make them one product: km (road, string chart, service band, mass), clock (fork, paper timeline, legal gauges) and latitude (metro, vertical corridor). Moving between screens means changing axis, not page.

Four rules hold it together: a decision sentence on top of every canvas; grey is normal and colour is exception; objects are drawn, not described; maps only as corridor or portrait, never as wallpaper.

What dies: the shell, the sidebar, tabs, the inspector, the map tile, KPI rows, card stacks and every table that pretends to be a screen.
