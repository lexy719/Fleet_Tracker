FleetOS — Phase 5.7I: Instrument-Driven Operational Workspace & Attention-First Productization
(Request as supplied by the user, 2026-10-06. Section headings and rules preserved; formatting normalised.)

Mission
Phase 5.7H successfully preserved and strengthened the semantic workspace architecture: Anchor; Active lenses; Lead lens; Semantic groups; Sparse interaction overrides; Spatial questions; Spatially adaptive map geometry; Temporary takeover; Selection relevance/invalidation; Exact Esc restoration; Cross-domain support; Adaptive information depth.
Keep those foundations. Do NOT reset the interaction architecture.
However, the visual review identified a much more fundamental product-design problem: the workspace still looks like one shared template with different content. Different domains have different data, but they still use the same visual container language: eyebrow; title; anchor line; controls top-right; divider; large metric; support card; map block.
As long as that structure remains dominant, FleetOS will continue to look like a polished wireframe rather than a finished operational product. Phase 5.7I must break that pattern.
The core design move is: Each lead gets its own operational instrument. Differentiation should come from the geometry of the work itself, not from swapping text inside the same panel.

1. Preserve the semantic workspace
KEEP: anchor; active lenses; lead; semantic groups; generic interaction contracts; sparse pair overrides; spatial questions; explicit map requests; temporary takeover; selection persistence/invalidation; real history frames; same-page recomposition.
Do NOT bring back: page-per-domain navigation; random topology generation; one card per lens; floating fallback panels; generic focus modes; fixed domain camera presets.

2. The shared panel template must stop being the default visual primitive
Current leads/supports repeatedly use: eyebrow, title, anchor, expand/close, divider. This repeated chrome is one of the main reasons the screens look alike. Remove or drastically reduce that structure. A domain should not automatically render as PanelHeader + PanelBody.
Strong rule: Do not let the visual component hierarchy dictate the product hierarchy.

3. Introduce one persistent Anchor Strip
Replace repeated Job context/header lines with one shared persistent context strip. Example: J-4217 · Porto → Madrid · Em trânsito · SLA ok · Planeado. It may also communicate: current anchor; lifecycle; plan state; priority; current active lead; quiet lens/context trail where useful. It must remain visually restrained. Do NOT turn it into: a conventional app header; a breadcrumb bar; a large toolbar. Its job is orientation.
Strong principle: State the transport context once. Do not repeat it inside every region.

4. Lead headings should state the operational answer
Do not make the main headline merely the entity/domain name. Driver — weak: "Miguel Costa"; better: "Pausa obrigatória até 15:35" with supporting line "Bragança às 14:55 deixa 40 min de margem". Fuel / Route & Stops — weak: "Abastecimento"; better: "Mirandela é a melhor paragem antes da pausa". The lead should foreground the decision or state the operator needs to understand.

5. Supports must stop looking like miniature leads
Replace support presentation with only three primary forms:
- Margin note — attached directly to the relevant place in the lead instrument (e.g. Truck range annotation at the Driver planned fuel stop).
- Fact column — compact structured facts; no full panel header; expand/remove controls may appear on hover/focus.
- Consequence line — a short statement explaining operational impact plus an action (e.g. "CMR disponível · identidade conferida — View document →").
Strong principle: Support information should attach to the lead's question, not open a second mini-page.

6. Driver gets a real operational instrument
Driver is fundamentally temporal. Its primary instrument should be a full operational time axis, not a large 3h42 metric. The axis should be capable of showing: current time / now marker; driving periods; other work; breaks; rest; next planned stop; planned mandatory break; delivery-window relationship; current driving spell; daily usage; weekly usage; legal thresholds; uncertainty/staleness of the hours baseline. Relevant thresholds may include 4h30 continuous driving; daily driving limit; weekly driving state. Do not imply legal certainty from synthetic values.

7. Driver data honesty
Production driver-hours state is manually entered and can become stale. Do NOT show 3h42 as if it were live telemetry. Show provenance naturally (e.g. "3h42 available · manual baseline · 06:30" or "baseline stale · entered yesterday"). Provenance should be typographic, quiet and understandable — not a giant badge. Driver qualification states must eventually support: licence; CPC; ADR; tachograph card; VALID; EXPIRING; EXPIRED; MISSING; NOT_HELD. Use synthetic values in DEV, but design the information architecture honestly.

8. Driver lead should no longer be dominated by one hero number
3h42 may remain important, but it should be a value on the temporal instrument. The primary graphic is time progression + constraints + next action, not a metric card.

9. Truck gets a fundamentally different instrument
Truck must stop looking like Driver with the order reversed. Truck is an asset/capability problem. Create a vehicle/combo elevation instrument (tractor + trailer side elevation) with meaningful annotations: overall length; height; axle positions/count; gross weight; load/capacity; pallet occupancy; trailer type; ADR equipment; emission class; current assignment; availability; maintenance/attention. Preserve and integrate the pallet strip idea.

10. Restriction relationships should appear on the Truck instrument
Height restriction: a limit line against the vehicle elevation. Weight: actual/assumed vehicle fact beside legal threshold. Axles: highlight the relevant axle rule. Support attached directly to the lead's geometry.

11. Truck readings must show provenance
Production fuel and odometer are manually measured. "31% / 530 km / 29 L" looks like telemetry — fix it (e.g. "Fuel 31% · manual · 06:30 · depot"). If range is derived from synthetic assumptions, say so. Never visually imply a live signal when there is none.

12. Rename/reframe Fuel as Route & Stops in the lab
Fuel-stop data is not currently a real production domain. Evolve the lens toward Route & Stops: fuel opportunity; breaks/rest; stop suitability; parking; detour; timing; restrictions; delivery-window impact; route consequences. Do not pretend the stop dataset is production-real.

13. Route & Stops gets a route-strip instrument
An abstract route strip (Porto ───── Madrid, 0 km … 560 km) carrying: pickup; intermediate stops; selected candidate; fuel candidates; break/rest window; delivery window; restrictions; route incidents; current resource position; destination. Detours may branch as short spurs. Not a replacement for the map; it is the operational instrument.

14. Route & Stops must foreground trade-offs
Show: arrival; route km; detour; fuel cost; required break compatibility; parking; restriction impact; available margin. Make the system recommendation visually distinct from operator selection and alternatives. Selection must NOT rely only on green fill.

15. Documents keeps the paper, but becomes a dossier system
Requirements ledger (issued; missing; cannot issue yet; void; draft where applicable), CMR, invoices, numbering/series, relationship to Job/resources. Snapshot vs current: production documents use immutable issued snapshots — distinguish issued snapshot vs current operational data.

16. Document preview should not consume half the workspace by default
Give ledger/status/history more importance. Preview can scale, become a secondary column, or expand via temporary detail takeover.

17. Restrictions gets a logic instrument
Start from "Does this restriction apply?" Represent conditions as ✓ known satisfied / ✕ known not satisfied / ? missing-unknown. Then show missing facts; exemptions; authorisations; legal source; validity window; verification state; operational impact.

18. Do not use "Compatible" as an overconfident generic answer
Production semantics: APPLIES; DOES_NOT_APPLY; UNKNOWN, with trust/verification state. The UI must be able to say "Unknown — first axle height missing".

19. Show evidence/provenance naturally in Restrictions
e.g. "DL 155-A/2026 · art. 2–3 · official · verified" or "interpreted · source updated 2d ago". Calm and readable.

20. Reserve the visual language for Plan now
Do NOT implement the full Plan lens yet. Plan is comparative (Route A/B/C aligned against common axes: time; cost; restriction impact; driver feasibility; empty km; margin). The visual system must not block this.

21. Base state becomes Attention-first
Answer "What needs me?" instead of "What transports exist?": BLOCKERS; ACTION REQUIRED; RISK; then IN PROGRESS. Calm; not a notification center.

22. Attention items should be direct entry points
e.g. "J-4217 · REPLAN_REQUIRED · route facts changed": selecting sets anchor, activates relevant lenses, chooses the lead and the spatial question. Expiring Driver qualification → Driver lead. Road restriction conflict → Restrictions or Plan context.

23. Base map should become honest fleet context
Known positions; simulated positions; unknown positions. Unknown must not be silently placed on the map. Simulated positions visually marked. Synthetic data explicitly in the lab.

24. Create a real visual language for status
Selection: ink outline / typography weight / subtle marker (not success green). OK: muted green. Risk: amber. Action required: vermilion. Blocker: deep red. Unknown: dashed or hatched. Stale: reduced intensity + clock/time. Simulated/demo: dotted typographic underline or equivalent. Must work across Driver, Truck, Route & Stops, Documents, Restrictions, Attention.

25. Provenance should be typography, not badge soup
e.g. "31% · manual · 06:30", "Driver hours · baseline entered · today 06:30", "Simulated location". Avoid a pill/badge for every fact.

26. Typography overhaul
A strong grotesk for general UI; tabular figures for time/numbers; optional mono/technical styling for IDs. Differentiate prose; Job IDs; plates; times; distances; legal references. Plates read as plates. IDs do not look like prose.

27. Remove most small-caps eyebrows
Reserve small caps for the anchor strip and rare group labels.

28. Fix wide-screen typography
Do not place label and value 1000+ px apart. Use label above value; local grouped facts; controlled label column widths; logical clusters.

29. Restyle the TomTom map
Quieter land; less saturated roads; restrained labels; stronger hierarchy for operational overlays. Not monochrome to the point of losing readability.

30. Stop using straight-line fake routes where road-following geometry exists
Use recorded TomTom route fixtures or existing restriction geometries. If necessary move the synthetic Job corridor to one represented by fixture geometry; clearly mark remaining synthetic geometry. A visually beautiful fake road line is still worse than a plain honest route.

31. Restriction geometry must follow roads
Do not draw RI-042 as an arbitrary straight bar. Use stored road geometry.

32. Make the spatial question visible
A quiet map-context label (e.g. "Próxima etapa · Amarante → Bragança · 112 km"; "Restrição · segmento A4 · access condition").

33. Reduce prominence of "Expand map"
Quiet icon/action; contextual spatial controls; low-friction keyboard option. The map's question is more prominent than Expand.

34. Rail redesign
Group by semantic group: Operation (Jobs, eventually Plan); Resources (Driver, Truck, eventually Trailer); Journey (Route & Stops, Restrictions); Assurance (Documents, eventually Compliance). Spacing/separators, not large labels.

35. Active versus lead in the rail
Active lens = subtle dot/mark; lead = stronger filled treatment.

36. Remove DEV tools from the domain rail
Stress, exception toggles, matrix/debug, Reset → DEV drawer or separate DEV surface.

37. Related-domain links must stop looking like a footer
Integrate them contextually: on the Driver timeline; beside a Truck fact; inside the restriction logic; beside a stop on Route & Stops.

38. Cross-domain transitions must reflect instrument changes
Driver → Documents: the temporal instrument recedes; dossier/paper/ledger emerges; anchor strip stays; supports attach to the new instrument.

39. Motion should explain the change of question
Lead transfer; support becoming annotation; map changing role; timeline becoming secondary; dossier entering; takeover expanding. Preserve map resize/camera choreography.

40. 1920 must gain structure, not whitespace
Driver: temporal instrument; next action; consequences in parallel. Truck: elevation; capability/spec; execution. Documents: ledger; document; history/snapshot. Do not center a 900 px workspace.

41. 1199 behavior
Fact columns collapse into margin notes; instrument remains primary; lower-priority detail moves into controlled scrolling; map shape changes appropriately. Do not convert everything into vertical cards.

42. Jobs collection must become operationally deeper
Synthetic lifecycle state; priority; SLA; plan status; REPLAN_REQUIRED; blockers; multiple stops; windows.

43. Selected Job becomes Transport Overview
anchor = job, lead = Jobs is presented as Transport Overview (a representation change, not a new domain).

44. Transport Overview must become richer
Lifecycle; plan state; priority/SLA; route/stops; cargo summary; assigned resources; open exceptions; documents status; next human action. One strong transport narrative, not a dashboard.

45. Design for multi-stop Jobs now
Pickup; delivery; intermediate stop; dated time window; completion state — influencing Transport Overview, Route & Stops, Driver, future Plan.

46. Cargo must exist visually
Cargo lines; pallets; weight; optional ADR; optional temperature — influencing Truck capability and Restrictions support. Do not connect production data yet.

47. Attention-first base state
A serious prototype with blockers; action required; risk; active work. Each item answers: What happened? Why does the operator care? What needs to happen next? Selecting one enters the relevant workspace.

48. Avoid notification-center aesthetics
Not cards with red icons, feeds or alert-bell pages. Operational triage: concise rows; grouped severity; strong next-action wording; spatial relationship where relevant.

49. Editing — design only, minimal implementation
No full CRUD. Edit on the lead's instrument where possible: Driver — move/replan break on time axis; Route & Stops — choose alternative stop; Truck — compare/replace assigned resource.

50. Pending-change bar
A bottom change-commit surface showing: what changed; consequence; whether Job becomes REPLAN_REQUIRED; whether uncertainty must be accepted; what event/history entry will be created; Confirm / Cancel. Lightweight prototype.

51. Record creation remains takeover-based
Not solved in this phase.

52. Data-honesty visual states
Every instrument must be able to represent KNOWN, UNKNOWN, ASSUMED, STALE, SIMULATED, UNCERTAIN, ERROR, LOADING, EMPTY.

53. Required visual scenes
Shared: 1 anchor strip; 2 rail with groups; 3 data-honesty visual system.
Driver: 4 time instrument normal; 5 next required break; 6 stale/manual baseline; 7 critical margin; 8 + document support.
Truck: 9 elevation; 10 load/capacity; 11 relevant restriction; 12 manual fuel reading/provenance.
Route & Stops: 13 route strip; 14 candidate comparison; 15 selected stop; 16 break/rest relationship; 17 restriction on route.
Documents: 18 dossier + ledger; 19 snapshot vs current; 20 selected document detail.
Restrictions: 21 logic tree known/unknown; 22 exemption/authorization; 23 restriction + actual road geometry.
Jobs/Attention: 24 attention-first base; 25 Jobs collection lifecycle/SLA/plan; 26 Transport Overview.
Cross-domain: 27 Driver → Documents; 28 Truck → Restrictions; 29 Route & Stops → Driver.
Map: 30 restyled TomTom map; 31 map takeover using road-following route geometry.

54. Side-by-side design test — contact sheet of Driver, Truck, Route & Stops, Documents, Restrictions without external labels; distinction from geometry, instrument, hierarchy, relationships — not headings.
55. Remove-the-title test — hide each lead's domain title; can you still tell the work? If not, redesign.
56. Data-honesty test — stale Driver baseline; manual Truck reading; simulated location; unknown restriction applicability; uncertain planning assumption — understandable without badge noise.
57. Map trust test — no final screenshot with route/restriction geometry that visibly ignores the road unless labelled illustrative.
58. Visual quality bar — "Would I be comfortable using this on the product website?"
59. Do not integrate production state yet — DEV adapters/fixtures; synthetic shapes derived from the production domain model.
60. Do not build Plan yet.
61. Engineering — preserve semantic tests, map persistence, Esc/history, selection invalidation, DEV isolation. Run full test suite, typecheck, lint, build. Targeted tests only where needed.
62. Visual review before completion — look for repeated panel chrome; giant metric repetition; green overuse; empty wide layouts; support panels as mini-pages; fake route geometry; weak typographic contrast; unclear provenance; Driver/Truck mirroring; unnecessary map prominence.

Success condition: the workspace looks like one coherent FleetOS visual system containing several distinct operational instruments — Driver recognisable by time; Truck by vehicle/capability geometry; Route & Stops by route progression and stop decisions; Documents by dossier/paper/ledger; Restrictions by condition/evidence logic; Transport Overview as the operational truth of one transport; Attention as human work remaining. Supports no longer look like mini-pages; the map belongs to FleetOS; road geometry is trustworthy; provenance is visible without clutter; unknown/stale/simulated values are honest; wide screens gain structure; narrow screens retain hierarchy; the rail communicates semantic groups; the anchor appears once; all 5.7G/H semantic behaviour is preserved.
Stop after DEV visual review. Do not integrate production state. Do not implement full Plan yet.
