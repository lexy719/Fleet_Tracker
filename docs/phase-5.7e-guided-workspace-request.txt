FleetOS — Phase 5.7E: Guided Dynamic Workspace
Goal
We are resetting the interaction model.
The previous approach became too focused on:
- autonomous topology generation;
- generic representation libraries;
- layout scoring;
- many synthetic variants;
- floating fallback panels;
- preserving old rectangles.
It produced complexity without producing a convincing product.
This phase must build something much more deliberate:
an extremely advanced but guided operating workspace.
Not random.
Not a dashboard grid.
Not one layout per page.
Not autonomous visual generation.
Instead:
carefully authored interaction paths that adapt strongly to available space, active context and selected entities.
1. Keep only the core shell
Base environment:
- small floating domain rail/sidebar;
- map occupies the main usable area initially;
- substantial clean white space is acceptable;
- no traditional header;
- no permanent large navigation panel.
The rail contains domains such as:
- Jobs
- Drivers
- Trucks
- Fuel
- Restrictions
- Documents
Clicking a rail item introduces/removes that domain from the current workspace.
The rail itself must remain small.
It is not the content area.
2. Domain opening behavior
When a domain is activated:
do not open a fixed panel in a fixed place.
The workspace should inspect:
- current active domains;
- currently selected entity;
- current lead/expanded domain;
- available white space;
- current map importance;
- content quantity;
- useful minimum/preferred size of each active domain;
- existing composition.
Then choose one of a small number of carefully designed valid arrangements.
Existing content may:
- move;
- resize;
- change internal arrangement;
- compress;
- expand;
- merge visually with related information;
- disappear if temporarily unnecessary.
Do NOT freeze existing surfaces.
Do NOT randomly reshuffle everything either.
Strong principle:
Highly variable, but through authored paths — never arbitrary.
3. There are only two main domain states
Keep the interaction language simple.
A domain has primarily:
Normal
The domain is present in the workspace.
Expanded / Lead
The user explicitly asks to work more deeply with this domain.
Avoid unnecessary concepts such as:
- focus mode;
- explore mode;
- fullscreen mode;
- separate detail mode;
- floating fallback mode.
A domain can have internal selected entities, but the main workspace interaction should remain understandable:
domain active
domain expanded
entity selected

4. Expand does NOT open another page
This is fundamental.
Imagine:
selected Job: J-4217

active:
Fuel
Driver
Truck
Documents
Restrictions

If the user expands Fuel:
the whole existing page recomposes around Fuel.
Fuel becomes the lead domain.
Driver, Truck, Documents and Restrictions remain part of the SAME page.
They do not open as separate windows.
They do not disappear unless intentionally irrelevant.
They do not remain detached around the lead domain.
Their representation changes so they support the current lead context.
Strong rule:
Expand changes which context leads the composition; it does not navigate away.
5. Anchor + active lenses + lead lens
Model the workspace explicitly with three concepts.
Anchor
The object currently being worked on.
Examples:
Jobs collection
Job J-4217
Driver D-14
Truck T-08

Active lenses
Domains introduced into the workspace:
Fuel
Driver
Truck
Documents
Restrictions

Lead lens
One active lens may become the primary perspective.
Example:
anchor = Job J-4217
active = Fuel, Driver, Truck, Documents
lead = Fuel

The anchor remains the same while the lead lens changes.
6. Supporting lenses must change meaning
When one lens becomes lead, the others must not merely become smaller versions of themselves.
They should answer the question created by the lead lens.
Example:
Lead = Fuel
Fuel becomes the main working area.
Supporting Driver may emphasize:
- remaining legal driving time;
- current rest state;
- whether the proposed stop fits timing.
Supporting Truck may emphasize:
- fuel level;
- range;
- consumption;
- relevant vehicle constraints.
Supporting Restrictions may emphasize:
- restrictions affecting the fuel stop or route.
Supporting Documents may reduce to only relevant documentation/status.
Map likely remains useful and may grow.
Lead = Documents
Documents becomes the main workspace.
Supporting Driver and Truck may become identity/status summaries.
Map may shrink dramatically or disappear.
Job/document state becomes more prominent.
Lead = Driver
Driver becomes primary.
Supporting Truck becomes tightly related.
Fuel may show upcoming fuel requirement.
Restrictions may show only relevant operational constraints.
Map may become important again.
Strong principle:
Supporting lenses should answer the question created by the lead lens.
7. Do not force one visible box per domain
A critical rule:
active domain does NOT mean:
one domain = one rectangle

Related information may combine naturally.
Example:
Driver + Truck might appear as one cohesive composition:
MIGUEL COSTA
Driving 3h42 remaining

DAF XG 480 · AA-42-ZP
Fuel 61% · Trailer PT-3821

If Truck becomes lead, hierarchy can invert:
DAF XG 480
AA-42-ZP

Driver
Miguel Costa · 3h42 remaining

The information remains connected.
Do not create card soup merely because multiple lenses are active.
8. Jobs should be the first fully designed path
Build Jobs first and use it to prove the architecture before generalizing.
We want several deliberate Jobs paths.
Jobs — normal / narrow
When Jobs has limited width:
Show a compact operational list.
Possible content:
- Job ID
- origin → destination
- status
- ETA/window
Map remains dominant if useful.
Jobs — normal / medium
With more space:
Add:
- driver/truck summary;
- progress;
- exception indicator;
- more rows.
Jobs — normal / wide
With significant space:
Internal composition can change.
Examples:
- richer list;
- two-column structure;
- grouped jobs;
- operational timeline;
- route summary.
Do NOT simply stretch the narrow version.
Jobs — expanded
A likely major state:
approximately:
Jobs workspace | Map

Often near 50/50, but not hardcoded.
Jobs receives much richer information.
Map shows the relevant jobs/routes.
Internal Jobs composition adapts to available dimensions.
Single Job selected
Clicking a Job changes the anchor:
Jobs collection
→
Job J-4217

The generic Jobs list may disappear or reduce dramatically.
The page becomes a coordinated Job workspace.
Map shows only the relevant job geography if map is useful.
Related active lenses remain in the same page.
9. One selected Job must support lens switching
Explicitly implement and demonstrate:
select Job J-4217
activate Fuel
activate Driver
activate Truck
activate Documents

Then test:
Expand Fuel
Expand Driver
Expand Truck
Expand Documents

For every expansion:
- same Job remains anchor;
- expanded lens becomes primary;
- other active lenses remain in the same composed page;
- their internal representation changes appropriately;
- map adapts based on relevance;
- no page navigation occurs.
10. Adaptive internal information architecture
A domain at different sizes must not be the same component resized.
Example Jobs:
300px
J-4217
Porto → Madrid
In transit · 18:40

600px
Add:
- driver;
- truck;
- progress;
- delivery window.
1000px+
May change structure completely:
- multi-column;
- grouped sections;
- route/timeline;
- exception areas;
- richer overview.
Do this using authored responsive compositions.
Apply the same philosophy later to other domains.
11. White space should actively influence composition
White space is an input to the design.
When a domain opens:
determine:
- where useful free regions exist;
- what shape they have;
- which existing element can reasonably give up space;
- whether the map needs to remain large.
The environment should make strong use of available space.
Avoid both extremes:
- filling everything;
- leaving half the screen empty while active information is compressed.
Strong rule:
Intentional white space is good. Wasted white space is not.
12. The map is contextual, not mandatory
The map remains a major FleetOS material, but it must not be forced into every state.
Use it when geography matters.
Examples:
Useful:
- Job routes;
- Driver current location;
- Truck location;
- Fuel stop;
- Restrictions.
Potentially unnecessary:
- document-heavy workspace;
- certain commercial/admin states.
The map may:
- dominate;
- share the page;
- become narrow;
- become a route strip;
- move;
- disappear.
Do not automatically use:
map fullscreen + floating card

That pattern should not become a universal focus template.
13. No floating fallback as default behavior
If the workspace becomes crowded:
do NOT simply open the newest domain as a floating panel.
Instead:
1. reassess all active domains;
2. choose lower-density forms where appropriate;
3. resize/reposition;
4. merge related information where appropriate;
5. reduce map area if geography is less important.
Floating UI may exist for specific interaction needs, but it must not be the generic no-space solution.
14. Designed paths, adaptive results
We are NOT building a fully procedural composition generator.
Create authored path families.
Example:
Jobs normal
↓
Jobs expanded
↓
single Job
↓
Fuel lead
↓
Driver lead
↓
Documents lead

Inside each path/state, geometry and information layout adapt according to:
- viewport;
- active lenses;
- data quantity;
- map relevance.
This gives variation without randomness.
Strong principle:
The system adapts within a designed interaction path; it does not invent the path itself.
15. Motion
Transitions must preserve visual continuity.
When something becomes lead:
- its current presence should naturally grow/recompose;
- supporting information should move/restructure;
- map geometry should change at the same time;
- map camera should reframe continuously if needed.
Avoid:
- instant panel replacement;
- resize then camera snap;
- disappearing everything and rendering a new screen;
- modal-like takeover.
It should feel like:
the same workspace reorganizing itself.
16. Esc / back
Preserve exact composition history.
Example:
Job
→ Fuel lead
→ select fuel stop
→ Driver lead

Esc should unwind real states:
Driver lead
→ fuel stop state
→ Fuel lead
→ Job

Preserve:
- anchor;
- active lenses;
- lead lens;
- selection;
- geometry;
- map camera;
- scroll where practical.
17. DEV content
Use realistic synthetic content sufficient to judge the UI.
We need actual:
- Jobs rows;
- Drivers;
- Trucks;
- Fuel stations;
- Documents;
- Restrictions.
But do not connect production repositories yet.
The purpose is to evaluate:
- information hierarchy;
- adaptive composition;
- lens interaction;
- expanded states;
- selected entity states.

Important implementation order:

Learn from the Jobs path first.
Extract abstractions only after the interaction proves itself visually.

20. Acceptance test: the critical question
For every screenshot ask:
Does this feel like the same FleetOS workspace intelligently reorganizing itself around what the operator is currently doing?

If instead it feels like:
- another dashboard;
- a modal;
- a different page;
- a floating card;
- resized rectangles;
- a generic grid;
then the interaction is not complete.
21. What NOT to build
Do NOT build:
- arbitrary procedural layout generation;
- topology diversity metrics;
- hundreds of topology hashes;
- 15 artificial variants per domain;
- random layout variation;
- generic floating no-space panels;
- fixed “focus layout” templates;
- a giant generic solver before proving Jobs;
- production data integration.
Success condition
This phase succeeds when:
1. the base map + rail feels calm;
2. adding Jobs uses available space naturally;
3. Jobs changes internal structure according to available dimensions;
4. expanding Jobs transforms the entire workspace;
5. selecting one Job creates a coherent Job workspace;
6. Fuel/Driver/Truck/Documents can coexist within that same Job workspace;
7. expanding any one of them makes it the lead perspective without navigating away;
8. supporting lenses change their information to support the lead lens;
9. map presence/size is driven by usefulness;
10. the whole environment feels highly variable but clearly designed.
Final principles:
Highly variable, but through authored paths — never arbitrary.
Expand changes the lead perspective, not the page.
Supporting lenses answer the question created by the lead lens.
A domain does not own a rectangle; it contributes information to the current workspace.
The system adapts within a designed interaction path; it does not invent the path itself.
The same workspace reorganizes itself rather than navigating to disconnected screens.
Stop after the DEV interaction lab is visually reviewed.
Do not integrate production data yet.
